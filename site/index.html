Why I built it

Someone is always starting their day while IT is asleep.

I've worked help desks for years. At a remote company, a simple question like "what's my username?" can wait hours for someone to wake up.

So I made up a company to try it on. Meridian has 230 people in 14 countries and an IT team of three, in Berlin and Toronto, with nobody in Asia-Pacific.

All the data is fake. Meridian doesn't exist, and nothing comes from a real employer.

Why you can trust it

The AI sorts. Code decides. People handle the risky stuff.

The AI only sorts the request

Plain code checks who you are, decides what happens and writes every reply. The AI never makes up an answer.

It can only see your own account

It has no way to look up anyone else, so even a fooled AI can't give out a colleague's details.

When in doubt, a person decides

Anything unclear, risky or broken goes to the IT team instead of a guess.

How it was tested

I tried hard to prove myself wrong.

A sealed, blind test

The test requests were written by a helper that never saw the code, and stayed locked until the final run.

Real attack patterns

Pressure, fake urgency and "just confirm their login" tricks, based on real help-desk attacks reported by CISA.

Broken on purpose

I made the AI's first step fail on purpose, to prove the backup checks still stop a leak.

Every number checked

Tests check every result in the write-up against the raw data. They caught three of my own mistakes.
